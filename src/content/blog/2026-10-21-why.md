Have you ever wondered why food tastes better in Asia?

Yes, the ingredients here are better. We use more spices, more chili perhaps. Something something more "umami". Some just say that Asians are better at cooking (perhaps a tautological claim)?

It seems to me that a commonly underrated factor is the climate here. You see, most big Asian cities are some mix of tropical and coastal. For instance, Shanghai, Taipei, Hong Kong and Tokyo share what's called [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate#Asia), while Singapore is straight up tropical. 

If you've visited one of these cities before, you probably remember being hit with a wave of humidity on touchdown.
The heat and humidity of these places help smells transmit better. Smell is a big part of the sensation of flavor, so better smells mean better food.

This effect cuts both ways. Food smells better and the smells transmit farther, so this is also my theory as to why open air food hawking is so much more prevalent in Asia (think night markets of Taipei or the hawker centers of Singapore). 

However, bad things smell worse. The humidity makes organic waste break down much faster, so uncollected garbage smells much worse!
