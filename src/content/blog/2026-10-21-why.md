Have you ever wondered why food tastes better in Asia?

Yes, the ingredients here are better. We use more spices, more chili perhaps. Something something more "umami". Some just say that Asians are better at cooking (perhaps a circular claim)?

But I think one commonly underrated factor is the climate.

Many of Asia's big cities are hot and humid. Shanghai, Taipei, Hong Kong and Tokyo all have so-called [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate#Asia), while Singapore is straight up tropical.

If you've visited one of these cities before, you probably remember being hit with a wave of humidity on touchdown.

Heat and humidity can intensify the smells around us. Since smell is such an important part of the flavor of food, I wonder if the climate itself makes eating here more enjoyable.

This may also help explain why open air hawking is so prevelant in Asia. Think of the night markets of Taipei or the hawker centers of Singapore. You can often smell the food before you see it. Perhaps the climate makes these places especially effective at drawing in hungry people.

Of course, this effect cuts both ways.

If good things smell better, bad things smell worse. Organic waste decomposes faster, which might explain why uncollected garbage smells awful in these cities!
