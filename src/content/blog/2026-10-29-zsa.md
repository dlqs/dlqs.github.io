A while ago, on the advice of an older engineer who told me about the importance of preventing wrist pain, I bought a [ZSA Voyager](https://www.zsa.io/voyager) keyboard.

At the same time, I decided to learn the [Colemak-dh](https://colemakmods.github.io/mod-dh/) keyboard layout. Partly for ergonomic reasons (it's actually easier on the fingers), but mostly because I wanted to see if I could rewire my brain on a tool I've been using practically my whole adult life.

Obviously, this made the switch to the split keyboard twice as difficult. But I figured that I'd get over the pain for both in one go, and perhaps it'd be useful for my brain to have a "trigger" to switch between the layouts: split keyboard + Colemak-dh, laptop + QWERTY. 

So for a while, my routine was: start my day with the split keyboard, try to send some messages with it (at a painfully slow 30 wpm), struggle for maybe thirty minutes, then switch to my regular laptop keyboard. This lasted for approximately two weeks. Then I put away the split keyboard in a drawer and promptly forgot about it for the next six months.

Then one day I saw it and remembered, "oh yeah I spent $300 on that", and pulled it out again. For whatever reason - maybe the Colemak neurons in my brain settled? - the second time was much better. When I hit roughly 60-70 wpm, I crossed the threshold where it could become my default layout, then I finally updated my laptop keyboard to match, thus completing the cutover. End to end, this took roughly a year.

So yeah. Split keyboards are great! Just don't pair the cutover with a layout change too, unless you're like me, and like to torture yourself.
