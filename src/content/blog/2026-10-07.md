Most marketing for acne cream is wrong.

The majority of acne creams are based on benzoyl peroxide, which "kills acne". Specifically, there are really two aspects to "acne": the bacterium _C. acnes_, and the medical condition acne vulgaris. Left untreated, _C. acnes_ causes acne vulgaris, aka "pimples", "spots" or usually just "acne".

BP works by oxidizing on the surface of the skin and killing off _C. acnes_. Logically, this means that BP must be applied before any acne forms.

However, that is not how most acne creams are marketed! Neutrogena, for instance, markets their [On-the-Spot Acne Treatment](https://www.neutrogena.com.sg/face/moisturisers/on-the-spot-pimpleacne-treatment) (2.5% BP) as, well, "spot treatment". But that is exactly the wrong framing. Spot treatment is a misnomer! By the time the acne has shown up, its too late.

Neutrogena is not alone here - there's a whole marketing category of "spot treatments" (e.g. [Oxy](https://www.fairprice.com.sg/product/398091) (5% BP)), which exists for reasons I do not understand. I suspect most people do not empirically evaluate the effectiveness of their acne treatments, or they falsely correlate the natural resolution of their spots with application of acne cream.

Either way: BP prevents acne, so the right way to use it is to apply it at _all_ acne prone areas, before the acne has shown up. For most people this will be the whole face, and for others, the neck, ears, chest, etc.
