What are the typical medicines you'll get?

First up, we have analgelsics - the pain killers. You probably already stock this some paracetamol/Panadol, which works by inhibiting pain signals inside the central nervous system. 

If you're American, you know this as acetaminophen/Tylenol, which is in fact the same; both names are abbreviations of the same chemical: para-acetylaminophenol. Either way, you almost certainly have heard of ibuprofen/Advil, another analgelsic has the same effect as paracetamol. 

So what's the difference? Ibuprofen works by inhibiting pain signals at injury sites directly, however, this comes at the cost of slightly elevated levels of damage to other body parts such as the liver, even used normally. While this effect is absent in paracetamol, it comes with the tradeoff of being more dangerous in overdose situations. Generally, paracetamol is considered the "better" of the two and the default in Asia.

When your body encounters substances it sees as threats, it releases histamines, which cause allergic reactions, like sneezing. So an antihistamine like ceterizine HCl/Zyrtec will help block these.

Antihistamines are classified into generations, by the intensity of its side effects, primarily on the brain. Ceterizine is actually a third generation antihistamine, since it crosses the blood brain barrier and causes drowsiness. If you want a fourth generation antihistamine, which doesn't cross the blood brain barrier, you can get levoceterizine/Xyzal, which is simply the left handed isomer of ceterizine.

It gets more interesting. Ceterizine in its regular formation is in fact what's know as a _racemic_, a mix of the two chirals halves of ceterizine: levoceterizine and dextroceterizine. So this is why Xyzal is able to achieve approximately the same effect as Zyrtec with half the dose (5mg vs 10mg). But that doesn't explain the whole story. If you're sneezing a lot, you may want an antihistamine. When your body encounters substances it sees as threats, it releases histamines. So an antihistamine like ceterizine HCl (Zyrtec) will help block these.

You may also get Xyzal, levoceterizine. This is simply left handed isomer of ceterizine, hence "levo-", Latin for left. (In case you were wondering, right is "dextro-"!)

Levoceterizine has the same effect of ceterizine without the useless other half of the enantiomer pair, which is why levoceterizine comes in 5mg while ceterizine comes in 10mg. But then why does xyzal exist? For money reasons of course: when pharmaceutical companies have expiring patents, they will come up with the same molecule but in different forms (like isomers, or isolating the effective half in an enantiomer pair), so that they can patent and market _that_ version.

By the way, ever wonder about the HCl at the end? This is because medicines are formulated as hydrochloride salts, which keeps them highly soluble in water yet shelf stable. Sometimes manufacturers may present the medicine without the suffix, they're usually referring to the same thing. 

Back to ceterizine. Ceterizine is a third gen antihistamine, which means it crosses the blood brain barrier and may cause drowsiness. So you might instead get a fourth gen antihistamine, fexofenadine (telfast/allegra), which does not make you drowsy.

What if you have a lot of phlegm? You may have lysozyme HCl ("Leftose"), a throat pain reliver. Lysozyme is actually found naturally in raw egg whites.

Another medicine to reduce throat pain is Difflam (benzydamine HCl). Difflam is a NSAID like ibuprofen, but applied directly to the site of the pain (usually the back of the throat), so its much more direct. 

Another medicine you may get as a decongestant is pseudoephedrine/Sudafed. Pseudoephedrine is sympathomimetic, meaning it simulates the sympathic nervous system to help narrow your swollen blood vessels, reducing the stuffed up feeling. In fact, another sympathomimetic drug is methamphatemine, which shares largely the same structure as pseudoephedrine. In fact they are so similar that pseudoephedrine is a precursor to methamphatamine, which is why you can only get it at pharmacies and need to present ID to get it.

To combat coughs, you might get dextromethorphan, an antitussive, a cough suppressant. 

Finally, to break down excess mucus, we have acetylcysteine effervescent/Fluimucil, which literally disolves mucus.
