By now I've been to a total of 30+ bachata lessons at 4 different studios in 2 countries. So I've decided to write down some of the features of what makes a good - as well as bad - bachata lesson.

Let's start with the good:
 - Enough time to practice. Practice is by far the most important factor of whether I actually learn something or not. Bachata (and other pair dances) are unique in that they aren't really practicable alone at home. So having sufficient practice in class is really important. As for socials, typically they aren't a good place to do new moves since it won't look great (and I'll embarass my partner), _however_, if other students in my class are in the same social, I get a lot of practice time with them, then _that's_ good. That's why I try to stack lessons with socials immediately after.
 - Getting to dance with the teachers. In classes I'm typically dancing with other students who are also learning, so neither of us really know what the "correct" way is. However, when teachers join the rotation, it's really useful for us all as an "anchor" to a source of correctness.
 - Low student teacher ratio. This one's pretty straightforward; more teachers means more eyeballs.
 - Naming. Name your moves! All of human knowledge is taxonomizing and naming, dance moves are no different. Names give us concept handles by which we can recall moves and discuss them with people. Examples: around-the-world, lateral hip roll, sliding door.

Now the bad:
 - Excessively pedantry, particularly second-by-second granularity. At timescales shorter than a second, I don't find instructions useful, since bachata music, indeed any kind of latin dance music, happens way too quickly for that to be useful. All the minutiae goes out the window at the speed of a real dance. Better to focus on getting it right at realistic timescales. 
 - Showy-off teachers. I've had teachers who "show off": they do some complicated set of moves when showing us what they want to teach, then ask us to "copy" that bit that they really wanted to teach us. If you don't wanna teach the move, don't show it! You'll only confuse your students.

Here's a laundry list of other little things to note:
- Space: sufficient space for the number of students
- Air conditioning: small rooms get hot quickly when you stuff them full of people moving around
- Familiar songs: really useful for learning since the music doesn't change
